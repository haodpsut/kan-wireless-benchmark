\begin{letter}{Professor Zidong Wang\\Managing Editor-in-Chief\\\emph{Neurocomputing}}
\opening{Dear Professor Wang,}

We submit for your consideration the manuscript ``When Do Kolmogorov--Arnold Networks Help in
Wireless? A Matched-Capacity, Non-Saturated Benchmark'' as a Regular Article in
\emph{Neurocomputing}. The work is authored by Phuc Hao Do (Danang Architecture
University) and Minh Tuan Pham (The University of Danang, University of Science and
Technology); I serve as corresponding author.

Kolmogorov--Arnold networks move the nonlinearity of a multilayer perceptron from the nodes
onto learnable univariate functions on the edges, and are widely reported to match or beat
MLPs at fewer parameters while remaining interpretable. The manuscript asks when that edge-wise
basis actually helps, and why. It is a study of a learning architecture: three signal-processing
tasks serve as the testbed that makes the question concrete and resolvable, and the object under
study throughout is the network family rather than any one application.

The contribution is twofold. First, a parameter-matched, multi-seed protocol with paired
confidence intervals, applied to four KAN instantiations against MLP, convolutional and
classical baselines, which turns ``KAN uses fewer parameters'' into a testable statement rather
than an assumption. Second, and the reason the result transfers beyond the three tasks, a
controlled experiment that identifies the variable governing the outcome: the share of the
target that is additive rather than carried by cross-coordinate interaction. On a synthetic
family whose difficulty is held fixed by a structure-agnostic control at the same parameter
budget, the KAN advantage decays monotonically with interaction, reverses sign in the
small-sample regime, and erodes without reversing when data is plentiful. The ordering holds
across all four instantiations and survives matching network depth on both sides.

The findings are two-sided by design, and several run against the architecture the manuscript
examines as well as against our own earlier choices: a symmetric hyperparameter sweep widens
the gap rather than closing it, one of our own baselines is shown to have been under-tuned and
the correction is reported, and one earlier comparison is redone as a paired test with the
interval it should always have carried. We believe an evaluation that reports what it finds,
in both directions, is more useful to the community than another favourable benchmark.

The work is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for
publication elsewhere. The authors declare no competing financial or personal interests. This
work was supported by Funds for Science and Technology Development of the University of Danang
under Project B2025-DN02-26. The use of generative AI in preparing the manuscript is disclosed in a
dedicated section before the reference list; no generative tool produced, altered, or
interpreted any experimental measurement. All code, data-preparation scripts and result files
that reproduce every reported number are openly available at \repolink, and the manuscript maps
each table and figure to the script and result file that generate it.

Thank you for considering this submission.
